The Basics
Medicare Doesn't Cover Everything

When Congress created Medicare in 1965, it was designed as a foundation — not a complete solution. Original Medicare (Parts A & B) pays roughly 80 cents of every approved dollar. The remaining 20% is yours.

For a routine doctor visit, that's manageable. For a hospital stay, surgery, or cancer treatment, 20% of a $50,000 bill is $10,000 out of your pocket.

Medigap — also called Medicare Supplement Insurance — is private insurance sold specifically to fill those gaps. It doesn't replace Medicare. It works alongside it, paying what Medicare leaves behind.

"Any doctor. Any hospital. Anywhere in America. No network. No referral. No permission slip."

The Medigap Promise

Unlike Medicare Advantage, Medigap does not create a private network. If your doctor accepts Medicare — and the vast majority do — they accept your Medigap coverage automatically. You never need to ask whether you're "in network."

Your coverage travels with you. See a specialist in another state. Get treated at Mayo Clinic. Visit a teaching hospital. Medigap follows you everywhere Medicare does.

The Case for Medigap
Why Seniors Are Choosing Supplement Over Advantage

Every fall, during Medicare's Annual Enrollment Period, millions of seniors are bombarded with advertising for Medicare Advantage — the private alternative to Original Medicare. The ads emphasize $0 premiums, dental benefits, gym memberships, and vision coverage.

What they don't emphasize: prior authorization. Network restrictions. Annual plan changes. Out-of-pocket maximums that can reach $8,000 or more. And the quiet reality that when you're seriously ill, a low premium can turn into a very high bill.

"When the diagnosis is serious, you want to choose your doctor — not be assigned one. That's the difference between Medigap and Medicare Advantage."

Licensed Medicare Counselor

Medigap's higher monthly premium buys something Medicare Advantage cannot guarantee: predictability. You know your costs before you need care. You choose your doctors before you get sick. Your plan doesn't change its benefits every January.

For seniors with chronic conditions, frequent doctor visits, or strong relationships with specific physicians, Medigap consistently offers better overall value — even when the premium is higher.

The math matters. A senior paying $150/month more for Medigap than Medicare Advantage pays $1,800/year extra in premiums. One hospital stay — one MRI, one specialist visit requiring prior authorization — can exceed that difference immediately.

Your Options
The Plans That Matter Most

Medigap plans are standardized by federal law. Plan G from one insurer covers exactly the same benefits as Plan G from another. Only the price and the company differ.

Plan G Most Popular

Covers nearly all Medicare cost-sharing: Part A deductible, coinsurance, hospital days, skilled nursing, and foreign travel emergencies. You pay only the Part B deductible ($257 in 2025). Best for people who want maximum coverage.

Plan N Best Value

Same broad coverage as Plan G, with small copays (up to $20 for office visits, $50 for ER). Lower monthly premium. Best for healthier seniors comfortable with modest cost-sharing.

HD Plan G Low Premium

Identical benefits to Plan G but you cover the first ~$2,870 yourself before Medigap pays. Premiums can be as low as $40–$60/month. Best as a catastrophic safety net.

Note: Plan F is no longer available to those newly eligible for Medicare after Jan. 1, 2020. Existing holders keep their coverage.

Enrollment

Best time to enroll: Your 6-month Open Enrollment window, starting the month you turn 65 and enroll in Part B. During this window, insurers must accept you at standard rates — no medical underwriting.

Miss the window and most states allow insurers to charge more or deny coverage based on health history. Timing is everything.

Medigap vs. Medicare Advantage: Side by Side
Both programs serve Medicare beneficiaries. Their structure, costs, and trade-offs are fundamentally different.

Choose any Medicare doctor ✓ Yes — nationwide ✗ Network only
Referrals to see a specialist ✓ Never required Often required (HMO plans)
Prior authorization for procedures ✓ Rarely needed ✗ Frequently required
Predictable out-of-pocket costs ✓ Very predictable Variable — can be high
Coverage when traveling ✓ All 50 states ✗ Local service area only
Plan stability year to year ✓ Guaranteed renewable Benefits & networks change annually
Monthly premium Higher ($80–$200+/mo typical) ✓ Often $0 or low
Dental / vision / gym extras ✗ Not included ✓ Often included
Prescription drug coverage Separate Part D plan needed ✓ Usually bundled
Out-of-pocket maximum ✓ Near zero (Plan G) Up to $8,850 in-network (2025)
